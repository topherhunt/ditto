# Italian curriculum plan

The Italian instance of [curriculum.md](curriculum.md). Each module lists its course id, its requirements, its grammar focus, its six lessons, and the lemmas it `introduces`. A module may use its own lemmas plus every lemma introduced by the modules it requires, transitively. For main modules, that means every earlier main module.

The lemma lists are the core-vocabulary plan and decide ordering. A module author may add up to about 10 extra lemmas when natural sentences need them, but only lemmas that no module in this plan lists. Optional modules may also introduce lemmas that a later main module lists, because main modules never require them.

## Lemma conventions

- **Nouns:** the singular. Nouns with a feminine form for people use the masculine (`amica` -> `amico`, `figlia` -> `figlio`). Distinct words stay distinct (`fratello`, `sorella`).
- **Adjectives:** the masculine singular. `-issimo` forms use the base (`bellissimo` -> `bello`). Participles used as adjectives use the adjective (`aperto`, `chiuso`, `sposato`).
- **Verbs:** the infinitive. A verb with an attached pronoun is one token and takes the verb's lemma (`dimmi` -> `dire`, `aiutarmi` -> `aiutare`). A verb used reflexively uses its `-rsi` form (`mi chiamo` -> `chiamarsi`, `chiamo` -> `chiamare`); these are separate lemmas. Every tense of a verb shares one lemma (`vorrei`, `voglio` -> `volere`).
- **Articles:** every definite form is `il` (`il lo la i gli le`); every indefinite form is `uno` (`un uno una`). Numeral `uno` shares the lemma.
- **Articulated prepositions:** the simple preposition (`al alla ai` -> `a`, `del` -> `di`, `nel` -> `in`, `sul` -> `su`, `dal` -> `da`).
- **Elided tokens** are one token (`l'acqua`, `un'amica`, `c'è`, `quant'è`, `dov'è`, `anch'io`, `po'`). They take the lemma of their last full word (`acqua`, `amico`, `essere`, `essere`, `essere`, `io`, `poco`).
- **Pronouns:** each clitic is its own lemma (`mi ti si ci vi lo la li le gli ne`), as is each stressed form (`me te`) and each subject pronoun (`io tu lui lei noi voi loro`). A surface that is also an article needs a sense in the lexicon (`la#pron`, `le#pron`, `gli#pron`, `lo#pron`). Formal `Lei` is `lei`.
- **Possessives:** the masculine singular (`mia` -> `mio`). Possessive `loro` shares the pronoun lemma `loro`.
- **Numbers:** each numeral is its own lemma (`ventitré`, `trentuno`). Compound numerals aren't listed below; introduce them in the module that first uses them.
- **Proper nouns** (`PROPN`: people, cities, countries, holidays) are never introduced and may appear anywhere.
- **Senses:** give a surface a sense key only when two lemmas or parts of speech share it (`lo#pron`, `fa#ago`, `vicino#noun`, `sei#num` beside `sei` = you are).

## A1

### 1. Al bar -- `it-a1-bar`

- **Requires:** nothing.
- **Grammar:** `vorrei` + noun; `un/una/un'`; `il/la`; `posso` + infinitive and `va bene` as chunks; object `lo` in `lo prendo`.
- **Lessons:**
  1. Un caffè, per favore
  2. Un cornetto
  3. Da bere
  4. Caldo o freddo?
  5. Quant'è?
  6. Il conto
- **Introduces:** buongiorno, ciao, sì, no, scusare, per, favore, grazie, prego, volere, prendere, potere, pagare, essere, andare, bene, ecco, uno, il, e, o, con, in, anche, di, me, lo, quanto, costare, euro, conto, carta, contante, caffè, cappuccino, cornetto, acqua, tè, latte, succo, arancia, panino, birra, vino, bicchiere, bottiglia, frizzante, naturale, rosso, bianco, caldo, freddo, due, tre, quattro, cinque, altro, niente, tutto
